Remaining upgrade capacity = personal project allocation + net project collections − existing project costs − variable costs − buffer.

Do not also count project collections in personal income if using this formula. Record existing ChatGPT cost either in the project budget or personal budget, consistently. Keep time savings and hoped-for revenue outside this cash calculation.

## Benefit benchmarks

Use a short experiment before assuming a tool pays for itself.

| Test | What to measure | Proposed decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Removes a real constraint | Specific blocked tasks, frequency, time lost | Document examples over 2 weeks; identify exactly how the upgrade resolves them |
| Improves useful output | Useful job matches, completed build milestones, or accounting cases passing validation | Choose one target before purchase; assess the same cases before and after |
| Saves time | Net hours saved after setup, maintenance, and reviewing mistakes | Value saved time at a deliberately chosen rate; seek benefits of at least 2× incremental monthly cost as an uncertainty cushion |
| Is affordable | Actual cash available and total commitments | Pass the cash-flow worksheet independently of any time-value estimate |
| Stays reliable | Failed runs, incorrect outputs, restore capability | Reliability needs may justify a purchase even without immediate revenue; name the failure being prevented |
| Can be reversed | Cancellation, export, downgrade, migration effort | Prefer a one-month trial until evidence supports a longer commitment |

Illustration only: If upgrading from $20 Plus to a $100 Pro tier, the incremental cost is $80/month. At an assumed value of $25 per genuinely useful hour saved, time-value break-even is 3.2 hours/month; a 2× benefit target is 6.4 hours. Saving those hours does not produce $80 of cash unless it creates collected income or avoids an actual payment.

For a paid product later: contribution per customer = collected revenue per customer − variable service costs. Customers needed to cover an added fixed monthly cost = that cost ÷ positive contribution per customer, rounded up. This covers the added tool only, not owner pay or every business expense. If contribution is zero or negative, more customers do not solve the problem.

## Review routine

Review monthly and when Job Scout first works end-to-end, when you start using it regularly, before the accounting project handles important records, before another person depends on a tool, and whenever income changes materially. This is a suggested routine, not an automatic reminder.

1. Record actual spending and meaningful constraints since the last review.
2. Update available cash and the total spending ceiling.
3. Select at most one new paid experiment so its effect can be judged.
4. Write its success metric, cost ceiling, review date, and cancellation plan.
5. At review, keep, downgrade, cancel, or defer based on actual use and benefit.

For variable services, use available billing controls and application-side usage limits. Confirm whether a provider's budget setting actually stops spending or only sends alerts.
